\begin{abstract}
We prove that the ratio of the upper to lower vertex-position number is
unbounded even among connected cubic graphs. A family of order $O(m^3)$ has
upper vertex-position number at least $m^2$ and lower vertex-position number
at most $O(m)$; the construction uses staggered connections between rooted
geodesic paths. The corresponding ratios for local monophonic position and
local mutual visibility are also unbounded. We realize every integer pair
$(a,b)$ with $a\ge2$ and $a\le b\le3a-1$, and settle the case $a=1$.
An exhaustive computation covers all 556,471 connected cubic graphs through
20 vertices, all connected graphs through nine vertices, and all connected
graphs of maximum degree at most three through ten vertices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and the three questions}
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected and connected. Following
Thankachy et al.\ \cite[Definition 2.1]{vp}, an \emph{$x$-position set} is a
set $S\subseteq V(G)$ such that no member of $S\setminus\{v\}$ lies on
any shortest $x,v$-path, for each $v\in S$. Write $p_x(G)$ for its maximum
cardinality, and
\[
 \vp^-(G)=\min_x p_x(G),\qquad \vp(G)=\max_x p_x(G).
\]
The observer $x$ cannot belong to an $x$-position set of size at least two.
For nontrivial connected graphs, maximum sets can therefore be taken in
$V(G)\setminus\{x\}$; $p_x(K_1)=1$.

Problem 2.4 of \cite{vp} asks whether $\vp(G)/\vp^-(G)$ is bounded over
connected graphs. Section~\ref{sec:original} supplies the full proof of our
original negative answer. James Tuite subsequently asked about bounded
degree, local monophonic position and mutual visibility, and realizable
pairs $(\vp^-,\vp)$ (private communication, 11 October 2026). We answer these
questions in Sections~\ref{sec:cubic}--\ref{sec:pairs}. The bounded-degree
question has a sharp qualitative answer: the ratio is bounded for maximum
degree at most two and unbounded for maximum degree at most any fixed
$\Delta\ge3$, including the cubic class. The two local variants are
unbounded. The pair classification remains partial beyond our stated
realization region and finite witnesses.

As Tuite pointed out, trees are already settled in \cite[Corollary 3.6]{vp}. For a tree $T$ on at
least three vertices with $\ell$ leaves,
\[
 p_x(T)=\begin{cases}\ell-1&x\text{ a leaf},\\\ell&x\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad \frac{\vp(T)}{\vp^-(T)}=\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\le2.
\]
Thus a new tree enumeration cannot address unboundedness. The exceptions
$K_1,K_2$ both have ratio one. The survey \cite[Sections 3.3 and 4.1]{survey}
places vertex position and monophonic position in their broader setting.

Two elementary facts will be used repeatedly. A sphere about $x$ is an
$x$-position set, since distances strictly increase along an $x$-geodesic.
If $V(G)$ is covered by $q$ geodesics starting at $y$, then $p_y(G)\le q$:
two chosen vertices on the same geodesic violate the definition. Also, an
integer function $f$ satisfying $|f(u)-f(v)|\le1$ on every edge gives
$\dist(u,v)\ge |f(u)-f(v)|$, by summing along a path.

\section{The original unboundedness construction}\label{sec:original}
Fix $m\ge2$. Start with a vertex $y$ and $2m+1$ otherwise disjoint paths
from $y$: $H$ and $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ of length $4m$, and $Q_j$ of length
$a_j=2m+2j$, for $1\le j\le m$. Write $H_t,P_{i,t},Q_{j,t}$ for their
vertices at level $t$, with all level-zero vertices identified as $y$.
Put $b_j=2m+j+1$, and add precisely the edges
\[
 H_{b_j}Q_{j,b_j},\qquad Q_{j,a_j}P_{i,a_j}
 \quad(1\le i,j\le m).
\]
Call the resulting graph $G_m$, and put $x=H_{4m}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:original}
For $m\ge2$, the graph $G_m$ has order $7m^2+5m+1$ and satisfies
\[
 p_y(G_m)=2m+1,\quad \vp^-(G_m)\le2m+1,
 \quad \vp(G_m)\ge m(m-1).
\]
Consequently $\vp(G_m)/\vp^-(G_m)\ge m(m-1)/(2m+1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every edge changes the level by at most one. Hence each ray is a
$y$-geodesic, and the level of a vertex is its distance from $y$.
The added edges preserve level, so no $y$-geodesic can use them: a path
to level $t$ of length $t$ must increase level at every step. Thus every
vertex has a unique $y$-geodesic, along its own ray. This gives
$p_y\le2m+1$, with equality by choosing the final vertex of each ray.

Let $M=2m+1$. Define $f(y)=M$ and, for positive indices,
\begin{align*}
 f(H_t)&=\min\{M,4m-t\},\\
 f(P_{i,t})&=2m+(t\bmod2),\\
 f(Q_{j,t})&=\min\{M,\max\{4m+1-t,t-(2j+1)\}\}.
\end{align*}
These functions change by at most one on ray edges, including those
incident with $y$. At the first kind of added edge the values are
$2m-j-1$ and $2m-j$; at the second kind they are $2m-1$ and $2m$.
Thus $f$ is 1-Lipschitz on every edge, and $f(x)=0$.

For $1\le i\le m$ and $1\le j<m$, put $z_{i,j}=P_{i,a_j+1}$.
Then $f(z_{i,j})=M$, giving $\dist(x,z_{i,j})\ge M$.
The path going down $H$ to $H_{b_j}$, crossing to $Q_j$, going up
to $Q_{j,a_j}$, crossing to $P_i$, and taking one more step has length
\[
 (4m-b_j)+1+(a_j-b_j)+1+1=2m+1.
\]
The $m(m-1)$ distinct vertices $z_{i,j}$ therefore lie on the sphere
of radius $M$ about $x$. The sphere observation proves the lower bound.
The order follows by summing the ray lengths and adding $y$.
\end{proof}

This proves the negative answer to Problem 2.4. The
\href{https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/9cba9d04fef654e1e09d10813836d884854c0b3f/D5/S3/Combinatorics/PositionSets/VertexPositionRatioRefutation.lean}{frozen Lean source}
formalizes this original family and the two global bounds. The results
below are proved here and are not asserted to have been formalized.

\section{Maximum degree three and cubic graphs}\label{sec:cubic}
We replace the common hub by a binary tree and replace each multiway
connection at $Q_j$ by connections at successive levels. The target
sphere survives because its chosen level compensates for this staggering.

Let $m\ge2$, put $c=m+1$, and set
\[
 L=2c(m+2),\quad R=L+1-2c,\quad M=R+c,\quad
 a_j=2c(j+1),\quad b_j=c(j+2).
\]
Let $k$ be the least power of two with $k\ge2m+1$. Take a full binary
tree of depth $h=\log_2 k$, rooted at $y$. At $2m+1$ distinct leaves
attach rays $H,P_1,\ldots,P_m,Q_1,\ldots,Q_m$, each indexed from
$t=1$ beyond its tree leaf. The $H$ and $P_i$ rays have length $L$,
and $Q_j$ has length $a_j+m$. The other tree leaves have no attached ray.
Add the edges
\[
 H_{b_j}Q_{j,b_j},\qquad
 Q_{j,a_j+i-1}P_{i,a_j+i}\quad(1\le i,j\le m).
\]
Denote this graph by $B_m$ and put $x=H_L$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:subcubic}
The graph $B_m$ is connected, has maximum degree three, and has
\[
 N_m=3m^3+13m^2+13m+3+2k
\]
vertices. It satisfies $p_y(B_m)=k$ and
\[
 \dist(x,P_{i,a_j+c})=M\quad(1\le i,j\le m).
\]
In particular $\vp(B_m)\ge m^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Tree vertices have degree at most three. All added edges have distinct
endpoints on each individual ray: the first $Q_j$ exit is at $a_j>b_j$,
successive exits are one level apart, and exits on a fixed $P_i$ are
$2c$ levels apart. Each such endpoint was an interior degree-two
vertex and receives one edge. Ray endpoints remain leaves.

Give tree vertices their depth and ray vertices level $h+t$.
Each added edge changes level by zero or one, so every original
root-to-leaf path, including its attached ray, remains geodesic.
These $k$ paths cover all vertices. They give $p_y\le k$, and the
$k$ leaves of $B_m$ give equality: a leaf cannot be internal to a path
ending at a different leaf.

Define $f=M$ on the binary tree, and on the rays define
\begin{align*}
 f(H_t)&=\min\{M,L-t\},\\
 f(P_{i,t})&=\min\{M,R+i+\min_{1\le q\le m}|t-(a_q+i)|\},\\
 f(Q_{j,t})&=\min\{M,\max\{L+1-t,t-a_j+R\}\}.
\end{align*}
Each ray function is 1-Lipschitz. At its first vertex it equals $M$:
for $H$ and $Q_j$ this follows from $L-M=c-1\ge2$, and for $P_i$
from $a_1=4c$. Thus edges to the tree also satisfy the required bound.
The identity
\[
 2b_j=L+1+a_j-R
\]
shows that the values at $H_{b_j},Q_{j,b_j}$ are the truncations at
$M$ of $L-b_j,L+1-b_j$. At the other added edge, the values at
$Q_{j,a_j+i-1}$ and $P_{i,a_j+i}$ are respectively $R+i-1,R+i$.
Indeed $a_j\ge4c$ makes the increasing term in the $Q_j$ maximum
dominant there, and $i\le m<c$ prevents truncation. Hence $f$ is
1-Lipschitz on all edges, and $f(x)=0$.

At $z_{i,j}=P_{i,a_j+c}$, the nearest point of
$\{a_q+i:1\le q\le m\}$ is $a_j+i$; its distance is $c-i$.
This remains true for $j=m$. Therefore $f(z_{i,j})=M$.
For the reverse inequality, follow $H$ down to $b_j$, cross to $Q_j$,
go up to $a_j+i-1$, cross to $P_i$, and go up to $a_j+c$.
The length is
\[
 (L-b_j)+1+(a_j+i-1-b_j)+1+(c-i)=R+c=M.
\]
All indices lie on the specified rays: $b_j\le L/2<L$,
$b_j<a_j$, $a_j+i-1<a_j+m$, and $a_j+i\le a_j+c\le L-c<L$.
The $m^2$ distinct targets form an $x$-position set. Finally,
\[
 |V(B_m)|=(2k-1)+(m+1)L+\sum_{j=1}^m(a_j+m)=N_m.
\]
\end{proof}

We now make every degree equal to three. Take two copies of $B_m$, writing
$v^0,v^1$ for a vertex and its copy. At every degree-two vertex add
$v^0v^1$. At every degree-one vertex $u$, add four new vertices
$c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3$ in a cycle in that order; join $u^0$ to $c_0,c_1$
and $u^1$ to $c_2,c_3$. Different leaves receive disjoint cycles.
No edges are added at degree-three vertices. Call the resulting graph
$C_m$. The root of $B_m$ has degree two, so $y^0y^1$ is present.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cubic}
For every $m\ge2$, $C_m$ is a connected cubic graph of order
$2N_m+4k$ and satisfies
\[
 \vp^-(C_m)\le3k,\qquad \vp(C_m)\ge m^2,
 \qquad \frac{\vp(C_m)}{\vp^-(C_m)}\ge\frac{m^2}{3k}.
\]
The ratio is unbounded among connected cubic graphs. These examples
have ratio $\Omega(|V(C_m)|^{1/3})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every old or new vertex has degree three, and $y^0y^1$ connects the
two copies. There are $k$ degree-one vertices in $B_m$, giving the
asserted order. Collapse the two copies to $B_m$ and each attached
four-cycle to its corresponding leaf. Every edge projects to an edge
or a stationary step. Consequently distances between vertices in
copy zero cannot decrease, and the embedded copy provides equality.
The $m^2$ targets from Lemma~\ref{lem:subcubic}, in copy zero, are
still on the sphere of radius $M$ about $x^0$.

Write $\lambda(v)=\dist_{B_m}(y,v)$. In $C_m$,
\[
 \dist(y^0,v^0)=\lambda(v),\qquad
 \dist(y^0,v^1)=\lambda(v)+1.
\]
For the second equality, the root edge followed by a copy-one
geodesic gives the upper bound. Any path to copy one either uses a
copy-switching edge, which projects to a stationary step, or switches
via a leaf cycle, whose projected portion is stationary and has at
least three edges. Thus its length exceeds its projected length by
at least one, giving the lower bound.

For a leaf $u$ with $\lambda(u)=\ell$, its cycle vertices
$c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3$ have distances $\ell+1,\ell+1,\ell+2,\ell+2$
from $y^0$. The cycle is accessible only through $u^0,u^1$, so these
distances follow from the preceding equalities. For each of the $k$
original root-to-leaf geodesics $P$, use three $y^0$-geodesics:
its copy-zero version extended through $c_0,c_3$, its copy-zero
version extended through $c_1,c_2$, and $y^0y^1$ followed by its
copy-one version. These $3k$ geodesics cover $V(C_m)$.
Thus $p_{y^0}(C_m)\le3k$.

As $k<2(2m+1)$, the displayed ratio exceeds
$m^2/(6(2m+1))$, which tends to infinity. Also $|V(C_m)|=O(m^3)$,
giving the order-dependent lower growth bound along this family.
\end{proof}

For maximum degree at most two, a nontrivial connected graph is a path
or cycle. A path of order at least three has ratio two, and a cycle
has $p_x=2$ at every vertex \cite[Corollaries 2.7 and 4.2]{vp}.
Thus the maximum ratio in that class is two. Cubic graphs belong to
every class with maximum degree at most $\Delta\ge3$, proving
unboundedness there. If maximum degree \emph{exactly} $\Delta>3$ is
required, attach $\Delta-3$ leaves at $y^0$ in $C_m$. The old distances
are preserved and the root geodesic cover increases by $\Delta-3$,
so the ratio is at least $m^2/(3k+\Delta-3)$ and is unbounded
for each fixed $\Delta$ with that convention.

Useful finite-order upper bounds follow from \cite[Lemmas 2.5, 2.6,
2.10 and Theorem 2.9]{vp}. If $n\ge2$, minimum degree is $\delta$,
maximum degree is $\Delta$, radius is $r$ and diameter is $D$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \frac{\vp(G)}{\vp^-(G)}\le
 \frac{n-r}{\max\{1,\delta,\lceil(\Delta+1)/3\rceil,
                         \lceil(n-1)/D\rceil\}}.
\end{equation}
For bipartite graphs, $\lceil\Delta/2\rceil$ can replace
$\lceil(\Delta+1)/3\rceil$; if $r\ge3$, the numerator can be improved
to $n-r-1$ by \cite[Theorem 2.11]{vp}. In particular the denominator
for cubic graphs is at least $\max\{3,\lceil(n-1)/D\rceil\}$.
The asymptotic maximum as a function of cubic order remains open:
our lower bound is of order $n^{1/3}$ along the displayed family,
whereas these general upper bounds can be linear in $n$.

\section{Local monophonic position and local mutual visibility}\label{sec:local}
For these observer-excluding local variants, assume $|V(G)|\ge2$.
We state the localization explicitly, retaining the different path
quantifiers in the defining papers. A monophonic path is an induced
path. A monophonic position set has no three vertices on any induced
path \cite[Definition 2.2]{mp}; the survey discusses this in
Section 4.1 \cite{survey}.
For a fixed observer $x$, call $S\subseteq V(G)\setminus\{x\}$ a
\emph{local monophonic position set} if, for each $v\in S$, no induced
$x,v$-path contains another member of $S$. Denote its maximum size
by $m_x(G)$, and put $\pmp^-(G)=\min_x m_x(G)$ and
$\pmp^+(G)=\max_x m_x(G)$.

Di Stefano's mutual visibility condition requires the \emph{existence}
of a shortest path avoiding the other selected vertices
\cite[Section 2]{mv}.
Its local version is: for each $v\in S\subseteq V(G)\setminus\{x\}$,
there exists a shortest $x,v$-path whose internal vertices avoid $S$.
Write $q_x(G)$ for its maximum size, $\pmv^-(G)=\min_x q_x(G)$ and
$\pmv^+(G)=\max_x q_x(G)$. We do not additionally require visibility
between two members of $S$; that would retain the global condition
instead of localizing it. These minus signs refer to minima over
observers, not to minimum sizes of maximal sets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local}
Both $\pmp^+/\pmp^-$ and $\pmv^+/\pmv^-$ are unbounded over connected
graphs. For the fan $F_n=K_1\vee P_n$, $n\ge2$,
\[
 \pmp^-(F_n)=2,\qquad \pmp^+(F_n)=n.
\]
For the original graph $G_m$,
\[
 q_y(G_m)=2m+1,\qquad \pmv^+(G_m)\ge m(m-1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $w$ be the universal vertex of $F_n$, and label its path
$v_1,\ldots,v_n$. Every induced $w,v_i$-path is the single edge,
so $m_w=n$. At a path vertex $v_t$, at most one selected path vertex
can lie strictly on each side of $v_t$: the intervening path segment
is induced, and two on the same side violate the local condition.
The only additional possible selected vertex is $w$. Thus
$m_{v_t}\le3$, and $m_{v_1},m_{v_n}\le2$. The neighborhood of any
observer is always a local monophonic position set, since an induced
path between adjacent endpoints must be their edge. Hence endpoints
have value two and internal path vertices have value three. Together
with $m_w=n$, this proves the asserted extrema, including $n=2$.

In $G_m$, every $y$-geodesic is the unique ray path, as proved in
Proposition~\ref{prop:original}. Consequently two selected vertices
on one ray cannot be locally visible from $y$, and the ray endpoints
give $q_y=2m+1$. The $m(m-1)$ targets on the sphere about $x$ are
locally visible from $x$: every shortest path to a target has all
internal vertices at smaller distance, so it avoids all other
targets. The resulting lower ratio bound is again
$m(m-1)/(2m+1)$.
\end{proof}

If one counts the observer in local mutual visibility, it can be
adjoined freely, adding one to every $q_x$. The same family then gives
the lower ratio bound $(m(m-1)+1)/(2m+2)$, so the unboundedness conclusion
is independent of this counting convention.

\section{Realizable pairs}\label{sec:pairs}
Necessarily $1\le a\le b$ when $a=\vp^-(G)$ and $b=\vp(G)$.
By \cite[Corollary 2.7]{vp}, $a=1$ holds precisely for paths.
Consequently the pairs with $a=1$ are exactly $(1,1)$ and $(1,2)$,
represented by $K_1$ or $K_2$, and $P_3$, respectively.
Every diagonal pair $(a,a)$ is realized by $K_{a+1}$.

\begin{samepage}
Thankachy~et~al.\ \cite[Lemma 2.3]{vp} also give the exact pairs
$(a,6a-4)$ for every $a\ge3$. The following construction realizes
a full interval of values for each $a\ge2$.
\end{samepage}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:pairs}
Every pair of integers $a\ge2$, $a\le b\le3a-1$ is realized by a
connected graph with $\vp^-=a$ and $\vp=b$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It remains to handle $a+1\le b\le3a-1$. Choose positive integers
$r,t\le a$ with $r+t=b+1-a$. Take a vertex $y$, independent sets
$A,D$ of sizes $r,t$, and a clique $B$ of size $a$. Put in all edges
between $y$ and $A$, between $A$ and $B$, and between $B$ and $D$,
and no other edges. The graph has $b+2$ vertices.

From $y$, the distance layers are $A,B,D$; every vertex in an
earlier layer lies on a geodesic to every vertex in a later layer.
Thus $p_y=\max\{r,a,t\}=a$. From a vertex of $A$, the layers are
$\{y\}\cup B$ and $(A\setminus\{u\})\cup D$. A selected vertex
of $B$ forbids the entire second layer; $y$ forbids $A\setminus\{u\}$
but is incomparable with $D$. Hence
\[
 p_u=\max\{a+1,r+t-1,t+1\}\quad(u\in A).
\]
From $u\in D$, the layers are $B$, $A\cup(D\setminus\{u\})$,
and $\{y\}$. Selecting a vertex of $B$ forbids both later layers;
in their union, $y$ forbids $A$ and is incomparable with
$D\setminus\{u\}$. Therefore
\[
 p_u=\max\{a,r+t-1,t\}=\max\{a,r+t-1\}\quad(u\in D).
\]
Finally, from $u\in B$, all other vertices except $y$ are neighbors.
The second-layer vertex $y$ is comparable precisely with $A$.
Thus
\[
 p_u=\max\{r+a-1+t,a+t\}=b\quad(u\in B).
\]
The formulas exhibit all extremal choices and exclude larger ones.
All four displayed values lie between $a$ and $b$, with $a$ attained
at $y$ and $b$ attained in $B$.
\end{proof}

The region in Theorem~\ref{thm:pairs} is not a necessary condition.
An explicit nine-vertex graph has $(\vp^-,\vp)=(2,6)$. On
$V=\{0,\ldots,8\}$ take the edge set
\[
 \{05,06,07,15,17,26,28,37,38,48,57,68\}.
\]
Here $uv$ means the edge $\{u,v\}$. The exact values in vertex order are
\[
 (p_0,\ldots,p_8)=(4,2,3,6,3,3,5,4,4).
\]
Exhaustive computation through order nine proves that nine is the
minimum order for this pair. The minimum orders for $(2,b)$,
$b=2,3,4,5,6$, are respectively $3,4,5,7,9$.
Connected cubic graphs additionally realize $(3,9)$ at order 18.
The complete classification beyond the proved region remains open;
in particular, these computations do not decide whether $(2,b)$
is realizable for any $b\ge7$.

\section{Exact finite computations and remaining bounds}\label{sec:computation}
Handle $K_1$ separately with $p_x(K_1)=1$. For $n\ge2$ and fixed $x$,
define the geodesic order on $V(G)\setminus\{x\}$ by
\[
 u\preceq_x v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \dist(x,u)+\dist(u,v)=\dist(x,v).
\]
This is a partial order: concatenating geodesic equalities and using
the triangle inequality proves transitivity, and a strict relation
increases distance from $x$. Its antichains are exactly the
$x$-position sets that exclude $x$. Dilworth's theorem \cite{dilworth} therefore computes
$p_x$ as $n-1$ minus a maximum matching in the bipartite graph of
\emph{all strict comparable pairs}. Using all comparable pairs, rather
than just graph edges, is essential to this formula. All distances and
matching decisions use integers. Polynomial-time computability of these
position parameters was already established in
\cite[Theorem 5.7 and Corollary 5.8]{vp}.
Indeed, matching edges concatenate into disjoint directed chains, since
each vertex has at most one predecessor and successor and strict order
forbids cycles; a matching of size $s$ gives $n-1-s$ chains. Conversely,
linking successive vertices in a chain cover gives a matching of size
$n-1$ minus the number of chains.

For completeness, a maximum matching also gives an antichain of that
size. Put $N=n-1$ and let the matching size be $s$. Start at every
unmatched left vertex and traverse unmatched edges left to right and
matched edges right to left. Write $Z_L,Z_R$ for the reachable vertices
in the two copies. No unmatched right vertex is reachable, since that
would give an augmenting path. Every reachable matched left vertex is
paired with a reachable right vertex, so $|Z_L|-|Z_R|=N-s$.
Identifying the two copies with their vertex labels gives $Z_R\subseteq
Z_L$: if $v_R$ is reached from $u_L$ with $u\prec_x v$, either $v_L$
is unmatched and is a starting vertex, or it is matched to some $w_R$.
In the latter case $u\prec_x v\prec_x w$ implies the unmatched edge
$u_Lw_R$, followed by the matched edge $w_Rv_L$.
Consequently $A=\{v:v_L\in Z_L,\ v_R\notin Z_R\}$ has size $N-s$.
If $u\prec_x v$ and $u_L$ is reachable, then $v_R$ is reachable:
their edge is either traversable left to right or is the matched edge
by which $u_L$ was reached. Thus $A$ is an antichain. Together with
the chain cover, this proves the matching formula directly.

There are 273,193 connected graphs of orders one through nine in the
second search, and 2,571 connected graphs of maximum degree at most
three of orders one through ten in the third. Their exact maximum
ratios by order are, respectively,
\[
 (1,1,2,2,2,2,5/2,5/2,3),\qquad
 (1,1,2,2,2,2,2,5/2,5/2,5/2).
\]
The data record every $p_x$, every pair frequency and explicit graph
witnesses. A fresh enumeration reproduces every graph6 input.
An independent exact antichain search recomputes all 832,235 graph
records and all 13,496,353 observer values, without using matching.
The tree formula holds on all 81 subcubic trees of orders three
through ten. Exhaustive subsets, induced-path enumeration, and
reachability in the shortest-path directed acyclic graph check the
two local variants on all 142 nontrivial connected graphs through
order six and fans with $2\le n\le10$.

The construction checks verify every edge of the Lipschitz functions,
every geodesic in the stated covers, and every selected sphere vertex
for $2\le m\le12$, with additional cubic checks at $m=20,40$.
For example, $C_{40}$ has 427,670 vertices, a sphere containing
1,600 selected vertices, and a cover by 384 root geodesics; its ratio
is therefore at least $25/6$. These are certified lower and upper
bounds for the two parameters, not computed equalities for those
large graphs. The realization formulas are checked exactly for every
$1\le r,t\le a$ with $2\le a\le15$, together with the diagonal
examples for $1\le a\le30$.

The proofs settle boundedness for each fixed maximum-degree class and
both local variants. The quantitative cubic growth rate and the full
pair classification are the remaining questions. In particular, a
finite maximum of three through cubic order 20 does not imply an
all-order upper bound of three.
